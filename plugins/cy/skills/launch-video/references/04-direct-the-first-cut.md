# 04  Direct AI to make the first version

The useful production process starts with a scene plan someone can review.

## Choose the workflow

Editing recorded footage requires selecting moments, preserving spoken meaning, framing and captions.

Building video from real UI and graphics requires a clear source folder, timed scenes, controlled movement and a product truth check.

Generating an illustrative shot may be useful for a metaphor or a scene you can't capture, but it should never invent real interface behavior or a customer outcome.

## Ask for three genuinely different approaches

I like comparing an outcome-first scene, a problem-first scene, and a guided task walkthrough. Some products need a different set. The idea is to make the creative choice visible before committing to a render.

## Make a Shot Map

| When | Viewer question | Source and quality check |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Opening | Why should I care? | Real result or problem. Is the promise clear on mute? |
| Demonstration | What actually happens? | Authentic UI recording. Are interactions truthful and legible? |
| Outcome | What changes for me? | Approved product result. Can a stranger explain it? |
| Closing | What should I do? | Relevant next step. Are date, link and CTA correct? |

For each scene, write down its intended time, viewer question, exact source file, visible action, claim and what you will check. The Shot Map becomes your production reference.

For Beam, I'd try opening with the window appearing, briefly showing how someone selects it, and returning to the result. I'd check that every step matches how the real product behaves.

## A prompt to use

I'm making a launch video for \[PRODUCT\] aimed at \[AUDIENCE\]. The outcome I need someone to understand is \[RESULT\]. Target runtime is \[DURATION\]. Target aspect ratios are \[FORMATS\].

Inspect only the real screenshots, recordings and graphics in the source folder. List exactly what each file shows. Do not invent UI states, performance numbers, customer examples or unrecorded steps.

Propose three scene structures. For each, give the opening three seconds, scene times, filenames, on-screen copy, movement, product result and likely points of confusion.

Recommend one with a specific reason. Wait for approval before rendering.

## Review the scene plan before rendering

Is the important result visible? Is it easy to understand on a phone? Does the scene order imply a real workflow? Are the labels accurate? Is each claim supported by an original source?

If you can't explain why one shot is necessary, remove it or change its job.

After approving the plan, preserve the source files. The render should be reviewable against the same plan and original material.
