# Google Developer Documentation Style — Principles

## Purpose and scope

- Write for an audience of software developers and other technical practitioners; the goal is clear, consistent technical documentation.
- Treat the guide as house style, not objective truth or an industry standard. It doesn't replace another style guide you already follow, provide a complete set of basic writing guidelines, or give legal advice.
- Use the guide as a reference for specific questions; for orientation, start with the Highlights, Voice and tone, and Text-formatting summary topics, and look up terms in the word list topic.

## Reference hierarchy

Resolve style questions in this order:

1. **Project-specific style** — follow guidance specific to your project or product first, including deliberate exceptions to the general guide and product-only terms.
2. **This style guide** — apply it when project-specific style gives no explicit guidance.
3. **Third-party references** — when neither of the above answers the question:
   - **Spelling** — follow Merriam-Webster.com (see also the Spelling topic).
   - **Nontechnical style** — follow *The Chicago Manual of Style*, 17th edition.
   - **Technical style** — consult the Microsoft Writing Style Guide, but check whether the guidance is Microsoft-product-specific before applying it.

- At any stage, check established usage: search your organization's existing documentation or a broad language corpus such as Google Ngram Viewer.
- Other tech style guides (Apple Style Guide, Red Hat supplementary style guide) may inform your thinking but are not part of this style.

## Underlying rationale for guidelines

- Most guidelines are driven by accessibility, localization, globalization, and ease of understanding; when neither of those forces a choice, one option among several equally good ones was picked purely for consistency.
- Prefer brief, direct answers over detailed explanations in reference content — readers usually want a quick answer to a specific question, and excess explanation clutters the page.

## Break the rules

> Break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous. —George Orwell

- Treat the guide as guidelines, not rules: depart from it when doing so improves your content. ✅ Recommended: use a hyphenated form of a term when that form is more appropriate for readers in your domain, even if the guide spells it as one word.
- Prioritize clarity and consistency for your specific domain and readers over literal compliance with any guideline.
- When you depart from the guide, apply the departure consistently throughout the document.
