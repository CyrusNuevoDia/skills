# 01  Decide what the video should do

Before opening an editing tool, decide who the video is meant to help and what they should know afterward.

A founder can easily describe the product in terms of features and screens. Someone new is usually trying to answer a smaller question. Is this useful for work I already do? What changes if I use it? Why should I believe that?

## Make the viewer's job specific

Name the situation in which someone would need the product. Include the current alternative when it explains why the change matters. For Beam, the starting point is reaching an app running on another Mac. The improvement is being able to use its window here.

## A launch film can do different jobs

Apple's Macintosh commercial made a different future feel important. Dropbox's early videos showed file synchronization working. Dollar Shave Club made a subscription offer memorable through personality and humor.

Those films made different choices about what mattered. A software demo may need real UI early. A film about a new category may lead with a belief or a metaphor. Neither is automatically right for every launch.

## Write a Video Brief

1\. Viewer and situation. Who has the problem and when does it matter?

2\. Promise. What should they understand or believe by the end?

3\. Moment. Which scene would you fight to keep if the film had to be cut in half?

4\. Evidence. Which authentic footage, screenshot, outcome or person supports it?

5\. Next action and quality bar. What should the viewer do, and how will you know the video communicated?

The first test is simple. Ask someone unfamiliar with the product to watch the key moment and describe what they think the product does. Listen before explaining.

## One product can need different stories

A viewer already struggling with remote desktops may recognize the benefit of Beam immediately. Someone unfamiliar with that workflow may need a brief example of what was difficult before.

The product hasn't changed. The viewer is bringing different knowledge and expectations. Use the same brief to compare alternative openings without assuming one works for everyone.

Write down the choice you make. You'll want to return to it when the first render is ready.
