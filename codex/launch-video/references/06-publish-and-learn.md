# 06  Publish and learn

A native X video, a website walkthrough and a webinar teaser have different jobs. Give each enough time to communicate the useful part.

## How long should your launch video be?

I've been looking at research on product demo videos because I wanted to know whether the advice to keep everything very short is actually useful.

The answer depends on the video and where someone watches it.

In a 2026 study of 1,750 B2B product demos ([https\://www\.motiontheagency.com/insight/b2b-product-demo-video-study](https://www.motiontheagency.com/insight/b2b-product-demo-video-study)), Motion The Agency measured age-adjusted likes per view on YouTube. Only 11% of eligible demos under 30 seconds made its top-performing quartile. For videos between 30 seconds and two and a half minutes, the share was 23–27%.

A separate analysis of 1,550 launch videos on X ([https\://doomers.ai/guides/product-launch-videos](https://doomers.ai/guides/product-launch-videos)) found that videos lasting two to four minutes from accounts under 10,000 followers had 546,000 median views. The 30–60-second videos from similarly sized accounts had 151,000 median views.

Those are two different measurements. One looks at likes relative to views. The other looks at total views. Neither tells us how much of these videos people actually watched or proves the longer videos performed better because they were longer.

I take a practical lesson from the contrast. A quick reveal can fit into fifteen seconds. A product with several important steps might need a full minute or longer to make sense.

For a launch, I'd choose a length that lets someone see the meaningful result. Then I'd make shorter cuts of the strongest moment for distribution.

If I wanted to know what actually worked, I'd compare both audience understanding and real engagement data from the places where the videos were posted. A high completion rate on a ten-second teaser and a high number of views on a two-minute walkthrough are different kinds of results.

## Make a family of cuts

Launch cut. Enough context to explain the product and lead to the next action.

Social proof cut. One recognizable visual change, in a format that reads at phone size.

How-it-works cut. Actual steps for someone already interested.

Follow-up cut. A useful first-versus-revised or before-and-after example.

## Measure the job, not just the exposure

X impressions, reported video plays and watch completion are different counts. A viewed post doesn't prove that someone understood the product or clicked to try it.

I want to know whether people correctly describe what the software makes possible, whether they investigate further, and whether they complete a meaningful next action.

Keep the source post, creative variant, timestamp and destination link with those measures. If you repost a winner, record when you did it.

## What to improve next

Maybe the hook needed to show the result sooner. Maybe a technical workflow needed more room. Maybe a promised benefit wasn't recognizable to people outside your team.

Treat those as specific questions. Make a revised version that tests one important change, rather than adding visual polish without a reason.

The next video should begin with something you learned from the last one.
